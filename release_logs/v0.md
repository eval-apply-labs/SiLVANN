# SiLVANN 0.x — what each release brought

The releases of major version 0, newest first. Within a major an update keeps the models and the conversations
working; the README carries the newest release's notes, and this file every one of them.

## v0.10.1

A fix: the UI's Options show a downloaded picture model as there, and offer it to start: in v0.10.0 a download of
Qwen-Image 2.1 finished with its download button still showing, and the model could not be chosen — in the
Docker app too.

## v0.10.0

Pictures from a prompt — Qwen-Image 2.1 runs as a server's model of its own: a message is a prompt, and its answer a
picture.

[Qwen-Image 2.1](https://huggingface.co/eval-apply/silvann_tq_D8_qwen_image_2.1) (`silvann_tq_D8_qwen_image_2.1`, 18 GB)
is a 7-billion-parameter diffusion transformer with Qwen3-VL 8B's text model as its encoder: the two at 8 bits, its
picture decoder at 16, each on the card in turn, written in the engine's words as the text models are
(`engine/src/packages/ai_qwen_image`; its programs `lisp/qwen_image.lisp`, the transformer's steps, and
`lisp/qwen_image_vae.lisp`, the decoder's layers). In the UI a message typed is a prompt: its progress is
shown beside the box — the prompt read, each step, the decode — and the picture is drawn in the conversation and kept
with it as a PNG. A message sets its picture's size, steps and seed (`--size 768x1024 --steps 20 --seed 7`), the
config's `image` the rest. `silvann_cli` saves the picture where it runs, and through the API the answer's text names it.
A 1,024 x 1,024 picture at 40 steps takes 146 s on an RTX 5060 Ti 16 GB and 572 s on an MI50
([`docs/users.md`](../docs/users.md#a-picture-model-qwen-image-21)). ⚠ Its licence is the Qwen Research License Agreement:
research and other non-commercial use only.

Checked against diffusers' Qwen-Image 2.1 pipeline from the same prompt and seed, on both cards: a block of the
transformer, its prediction and six steps of it, the encoder's rows, the decoder, and the whole pipeline's latents and
pictures — each nearer diffusers' float32 than its own bfloat16 pipeline is. On the RTX 5060 Ti, through the server's
API and in a browser: the first conversation opened by itself, a prompt typed, its progress, the picture, the page
reloaded and the picture drawn again, a second picture.

The chat, after a user test: an answer is drawn as it comes without drawing the conversation again; while the model
thinks the answer says "thinking…", and **show thinking** shows the thought in a section of its own; a run of the
model's tool calls is a section, closed until you open it; a model with no conversation saved opens one as its server
starts, and a message typed brings it online without **Activate**. A picture model's header has no thinking or tool
switches.

On NVIDIA cards the CUDA family has its own kernels for what a picture needs: attention with a limit to each row's keys,
matrices at 16 bits (the decoder's 3 x 3 convolutions 30 to 48 times faster than the engine's generic kernel), a
convolution's rows, and an activation's rotation row by row before an 8-bit product. The 16-bit matrices and the
rotation serve the Qwen models' vision tower too: the 27B's reads a 1,024 x 1,024 picture in 0.65 s and a 2,048 x
2,048 one in 6.3 s on the RTX 5060 Ti. The AMD family has the first of them now as well, and reads a prompt's
DeltaNet rows without spilling registers: the 27B's 2,097-position prompt 3% faster on an MI50.
On Windows, NVIDIA's driver lets a model larger than the card start on it and then read the rest over PCIe at every
token: the CUDA family now refuses an allocation past the card's free memory, which has not yet
been checked on Windows; until it is, [`docs/users.md`](../docs/users.md#on-windows-the-docker-image) says which config
keeps a model within the card.

## v0.9.0

Tool calling — a model opens a Lisp REPL on the machine it runs on, in its answer.

The model calls one tool, `lisp`, in the format it was trained on — Qwen's and GLM's `<tool_call>`, Ministral's
`[TOOL_CALLS]` — with a form to evaluate: the answer stops where the call ends, the form runs in a REPL beside the
server, and its value is read back in as the model's chat template frames a tool's answer, the answer going on from
there. `(help)` lists the commands: `ls`, `read`, `write`, `cd` and `pwd` for the folders; `bash`, a shell command in
the working folder (stopped, with what it started, after 60 seconds — up to 600 when the form asks — or after 1 MB of
output); `fetch`, a web page's words without its markup; and Lisp to put them together — `define`, `let`, `lambda`,
`map`, `filter`, text and lists. The UI shows a run of calls as one `<!tools>` block ([`docs/users.md`](../docs/users.md#the-models-tool)).

What a command may do without asking you is the conversation's, chosen beside its **tools** switch: ask first — reads in
the workspace and in folders you allowed; reads unasked — every read, the default (a config's `tools`, `"chill"`); full
auto — everything. Anything else stops the answer with a question under it — allow the folder, allow once, or refuse —
and a refusal is said to the model, which answers without it. The switch works as the thinking switch does: off, the
model's calls are answered that the tool is off; a conversation's first message declares the tool, so one begun without
it does not take it on later. `"tools": "no"` in a config turns it off for every conversation on that server. The REPL
starts in `workspace/` beside the conversations (`tools_folder` names another) and runs as the server's user — in the
Docker image, the container's.

In a terminal, `silvann_cli` (`silvann_cli.py`, in the release from this one) has the conversation the UI has — over
ssh, say: the answer written as it comes, its thinking dimmed, the tool's calls and their values shown, and an ask
answered at a prompt — `y` once, `f` the folder (a read), `n` or Enter refuses. It takes the server's defaults unless
`--notools` or `--nothink` says otherwise, and `/mode` chooses what the tool may do
([`docs/users.md`](../docs/users.md#3-serve-it-and-chat)).

Checked through the server's API on two MI50s: the 27B, the 35B-A3B, the 122B (`D4E4`), GLM 5.3 Flash and Ministral 3
14B (`D8`) each found a word in a note in its workspace through the tool; the 27B, the 35B-A3B, the 122B and GLM asked
before reading a file outside it and went on once allowed; the 27B told the user of a write refused, and the 122B's
`(bash "wc -l list.md")` ran once allowed. A call and its answer are framed as each model's chat template frames them,
to the token. The terminal's chat, on the 35B-A3B: a read outside the workspace unasked in "chill", a `bash` asked
and allowed with `y`, a read asked and refused with `n`, an answer stopped with Ctrl-C, and a conversation put away
after two idle minutes answering again. On an RTX 5060 Ti the same through the server for the 27B, the 35B-A3B, the
122B and GLM, and for the 27B and the 35B-A3B a `fetch` and a write asked too; the terminal's chat the same on the
35B-A3B; the UI's switch, its selector and the three answers to an ask clicked in a browser; and inside the Docker
image, the tool on the volume's `workspace/` and `docker exec -it silvann silvann_cli`.

⚠ The models have new names — `silvann_tq_<bits>_<model>`, the model's name in lowercase:
`silvann_tq_D8E4_qwen3.6_35b_a3b`, `silvann_tq_D4_qwen3.8_27b`, `silvann_tq_D4E4_glm_5.3_flash`,
`silvann_tq_D8_ministral_3_14b` and the rest (`silvann list`). A model downloaded with an earlier release keeps its
folder and its old name, and runs as before, its conversations with it; but `silvann list` and a download know only the
new names, and a later release's new program files reach a model under its new name alone — download it again under
that name when you can (the conversations stay with the old folder). The repositories on Hugging Face were renamed, and
their old names lead to the new ones, so an earlier release still downloads.

Also: two servers started on one machine boot one after the other, each trying the card as the other leaves it. A setup
kept in `autotune.json` is chosen again when the card holds 1 GB less than when it was chosen, and on an AMD card a kept
setup is taken at the next start again — the card's memory in use, which changes from one boot to the next, had kept it
from matching; one the card refuses is chosen again around the config's own settings. An integrated GPU
(`khronos_opencl1_igpu`) reads pictures on the CPU unless the config's `picture_pixels` gives them room in its pool. The
UI starts again the model it last started only when that start booted. An install's `.gitignore` leaves out the Hugging
Face token, the UI's last start and the tool's files.

## v0.8.0

Pipeline parallelism, and a model's setup chosen for the machine it starts on.

A ring of machines: a model's layers split over several PCs, each holding a run of them, each server started from its
own config — `pipeline_parallelism` "yes", its `pipeline_parallelism_layers` and `pipeline_parallelism_next`, the
machine after it; the one whose layers begin at 0 answers the UI, the one with the last layers runs the head
([`docs/users.md`](../docs/users.md)). A model larger than one machine's RAM or card runs over several: GLM 5.3 Flash
over two stages, its experts in their RAM, gives the whole model's tokens, 33 of 33, at 6.95 tokens a second on two
MI50s; the 27B over two stages the same, 33 of 33. A config's `card` picks a machine's card, or `"cpu"` its CPU and RAM
— so a card and the CPU beside it are two machines of one ring: on the RTX 5060 Ti, the 35B-A3B half on the card and
half in RAM answers as Hugging Face's does (7 of 8 tokens, then its second at its own near-tie) at 13.8 tokens a second.
What crosses the network is a row of numbers a position. A Qwen model's pictures are read on one machine for now.

A config that names no placement — where the experts sit, how much of the card holds the ones used most, how long a
conversation the card has room for — gets one when the server starts: the setups are tried on the card fastest first
(one the card cannot hold is refused in about 2 seconds), the RAM is measured — a container's or a service's memory
limit counts, and 500 MB is left spare beside what the server holds — and the choice is kept in the model's folder,
`autotune.json`, so the next start takes it without trying ([`docs/users.md`](../docs/users.md#4-the-models-settings)).
`max_context` "auto" fills the card, up to the model's own length; `card_experts` "yes" or "no" keeps or leaves out the
card's tier, sized in 256 MB steps; a placement the config names is started as it is. On an MI50 it gives the 122B with
80 GB of RAM a 25 GB tier — 24.6 tokens a second, as the 24 GB given by hand — and GLM with 150 GB a 21 GB tier, 9.2-9.4
against 7.9-8.1; on an RTX 5060 Ti with 28 GB of RAM, the 35B-A3B an 11 GB tier, 57 tokens a second, and the 122B and
GLM their experts from the disk, as their rows of the list. A server that cannot start says why, in one line in the UI's
Options, with the setting to change. When RAM cannot hold two conversations at the full context, the one put aside is
saved to its folder and read back when it returns.

A video is read to its budget: the frames the processor samples, each fitted as it is read — a minute of 1080p held 0.39
GB at the peak, from 10.48 — and `max_tokens_per_video` (8,192 by default) sets how many tokens one becomes. With the
experts on the CPU the server no longer grows as it answers (40 rounds had added half a gigabyte; now flat), and its
answers are the same to the bit.

The Docker image keeps what it holds across an update: a test updates a running app from one image to a newer one of the
same tag, over the same volume, and checks that the model answers, that the conversations — a picture's and a video's
among them — come back with their history and go on, and that the video reader and the token are still there; it passed
from v0.7.0's image to this one, and runs before every release. Its data is a folder: the desktop's **SiLVANN data**
(`silvann_open_data`) opens it in Explorer, and a model downloaded on another machine is pasted into its `models`; the
image gives everything in it to the app's user each time it starts. `silvann_reclaim_space` gives Windows back the room
a deleted model leaves in Docker Desktop's disk file. The Windows scripts make their folder whole at each run — an
action or a desktop shortcut an older install lacks. `SILVANN_NAME` and `SILVANN_VOLUME` keep a second SiLVANN beside
the first.

## v0.7.0

Pictures. The Qwen models read one — the 27B, the 35B-A3B and the 122B, whose packs carry the vision
tower: in the UI the 📎 button attaches it to a message, and over the server's API `/cartridges/<id>/images` reads it
and a message names it. The picture is prepared as Hugging Face's processor prepares it and read by the model's tower;
its tokens take the model's three-part positions (time, row and column: M-RoPE), which the library computes in a word of
its own. The tower runs on the card, in the library's words — LayerNorm, GELU, attention over a set of patches, their
two-axis rotary — over the pack's own codes, with the CUDA family's set attention on the tensor cores and the AMD
family's own: pictures up to 4 megapixels (2048 x 2048, 4,096 tokens), from a quarter of a second for a small one to
about 15 s at the largest on an RTX 5060 Ti, and from 0.13 s to about 8.5 s on an MI50. Its weights (0.6 GB) stay on the card when the experts are on the CPU and in RAM otherwise, and its working
memory on the card is sized at boot for the largest picture — the 27B at its default context still fits a 16 GB card
(`picture_pixels`, `picture_weights`: [`docs/users.md`](../docs/users.md)). Checked against Hugging Face on the same
weights: the tower's output within 0.5% (Hugging Face's own tower in bfloat16 is 2.5% from its float32), and over a
picture's prompt the first attention layer within 6e-4. A conversation keeps its pictures, and is saved and brought back
with them. GLM 5.3 Flash reads pictures too, through the tower its pack carries (24 blocks of its own design, in the
library's words on the card: 0.25 s for 448 x 448, 12 s for 2044 x 2044 on an MI50; within 0.92% of Hugging Face's
float32, whose own bfloat16 is 7.6% away). Ministral 3 reads none: its pack carries no tower.

Videos, for the Qwen models and GLM: the same button attaches one — an animated GIF, WebP or PNG as it is, any other
video file through PyAV, which `install.sh` installs from `requirements-nonfree.txt` and the Docker image leaves out
(its scripts' `install_nonfree` adds it: [`docker/README.md`](../docker/README.md)). Its frames are taken at 2 a second, two frames read
together as one picture by the tower, each pair's time written into the message as the model's processor writes it; a
video is made at most half the conversation's context in tokens. Checked against Hugging Face's processor and tower on
the same weights — the frames, their times, the prompt to the token, the Qwen models' positions, the tower's rows
within 6e-4 — and a 6-second video's digits named in order by the 27B, the 35B-A3B and GLM.

Files, for every model: a Word document (`.docx`), a PDF (through pypdf, which the install brings) or a text file
(Markdown, CSV, JSON, code), attached with the same button, is read when it is attached, and its text goes into the
message before what you type, cut with a note to what the conversation's context still holds.

A model's program travels with it: the pictures' part is a file of its own, `lisp/qwen3_5_pictures.lisp`, which a folder fetched
before this release lacks — it reads text as before and refuses pictures until `silvann download <model>` fetches the
file.

A Docker image, for Windows above all ([`docker/README.md`](../docker/README.md)). It is the release, its environment and
its build for an NVIDIA card or the CPU, with the chat as the app in the browser; scripts install, update, start, stop,
export and import it — the models and the conversations included, so a copy on one drive starts again on another. It is
not published yet: `docker build -f docker/Dockerfile .` builds it. It has run on Linux, where the 27B and the 35B
answer in it as Hugging Face does, at the speed they have outside it; not yet on Windows. The UI downloads a model too:
its Options list the models this release fetches, download one with its progress and start it; a Hugging Face token
saved there, or with `silvann token --set`, makes the downloads faster. `SILVANN_DATA` puts the models and the
conversations in a folder of their own. And on a machine of two processors the runtime keeps itself out of the kernel's
NUMA balancing, whose sampling cost a card's run a few percent: on an MI50 in a two-socket server the 35B answers at 90
tokens a second, from 87.5, and reads a prompt at 199 positions a second, from 194.5; the card rows above are this
release's.

## v0.6.0

A family for old integrated GPUs, `khronos_opencl1_igpu`: the library's kernels as OpenCL C 1.2, for
a GPU whose OpenCL has neither shared virtual memory nor the generic address space. The build works out every pointer's
address space itself, so the library's source names none. It has run on one device so far, the RTX 5060 Ti through
NVIDIA's OpenCL: the library's checks pass, and the 27B answers as Hugging Face does, at 1.6 tokens a second. On a card
with memory of its own, the RAM the card reads crosses the bus every token; an integrated GPU, whose memory is the
machine's, is what it is for. It takes a device only once the checks have passed on it, and so far that is the 5060 Ti
alone, which the CUDA family takes first: no integrated GPU runs it yet
([`engine/src/silicon_families/khronos_opencl1_igpu/README.md`](../engine/src/silicon_families/khronos_opencl1_igpu/README.md)).
And a model holds less of the machine's RAM: it loads its output head and its embedding table before its layers and
keeps no copy of its own once the engine has them. Ministral 3 14B on the CPU alone holds 7.4 GB, from 10.0, and needs
7.4 GB while it loads, from 10.6; with a card, the 27B holds 0.3 GB of the machine's RAM, from 5.9 (beside the pack's
pages the system caches, which it gives back when it needs them).

## v0.5.1

The library's own attention, which runs wherever a family has no kernel of its own for it — a
prompt's on AMD cards and through OpenCL — reads a prompt's scores four rows at a time, each key once for the four: on
one MI50 the 27B's scores over a 256-row chunk take 6.2 ms, from 19, and the 27B reads a prompt at 101 positions a
second, from 96 (80 from 76 over 6,033 positions). Past the 4,352 positions the cache keeps at full precision, its
scores over the 8- and 4-bit tiers read eight bytes of a key at once: through OpenCL the 27B then answers at 6.6 tokens
a second, from 6.1, and reads that prompt at 28.7 positions a second, from 26.9. On the CPU the matrix products of a
prompt keep the four sums a token's products keep, one pass each: the 35B on the four-core Xeon alone reads a prompt at
27.6 positions a second, from 20.8 (with AVX2 alone 18.1, from 11.7), the 27B at 3.6, from 2.1 — and with AVX2 a
prompt's products now add in the order a token's do. GLM boots on any card's family, an NVIDIA card's too. One
configuration gives one answer to the bit, run after run; a prompt read as rows is checked against the same prompt a
position at a time with a bound ([`docs/developers.md`](../docs/developers.md)). Fixes: on AVX-512 an empty group of
experts read past its rows; the CPU's pool started no threads where `/sys` gives no topology; a launch over no blocks is
no longer made on NVIDIA and AMD cards, and a launch a card refuses is named. GLM with its experts read from an NVMe
disk, on an RTX 5060 Ti with 32 and 60 GB of RAM: [`docs/results.md`](../docs/results.md).

## v0.5.0

NVIDIA cards. The CUDA family ran on a card for the first time, an RTX 5060 Ti, and runs the matrix
products of a prompt, its attention and the experts on the card's tensor cores, and a token's matrix products through
them too: the 27B reads a prompt at 549 positions a second, from 19 with the library's generic kernels, and answers at
24 tokens a second, from 7.8 — past the 4,352 positions the cache keeps at full precision a chunk of the prompt attends
in a launch, not a row at a time (the 27B over 6,033 positions, 370 to 472 a second); every pack but GLM's is checked
against Hugging Face on it. Ministral reads a prompt as rows on every card, through the library's own attention over
many rows (958 positions a second on the 5060 Ti, from 34 a position at a time). On the CPU, a processor with AVX-512
runs the matrix products with it, and the CPU family has its own attention and DeltaNet convolution: beside the 5060 Ti
the 35B with its experts on the CPU answers at 28 tokens a second, from 15-17, and on the four-core Xeon alone at 9.0
on a short prompt, from 1.7, reading a prompt at 21 positions a second. And a fix: the CPU's pool of threads left out
the CPU the caller ran on rather than its core, so in about half of all runs the caller shared a core with a worker
(7.6 tokens a second against 9.0, the 35B on the CPU alone).

And on AMD cards, past the 4,352 positions the cache keeps at full precision, the attention over its 8-bit and 4-bit
tiers runs the family's own kernels: on one MI50 the 35B answers after a 6,033-position prompt at 59-63 tokens a
second, from 26, and reads that prompt at 144 positions a second, from 94. GLM and Ministral now make their answers on
the card as Qwen does, eight tokens a call (`lisp/generate.lisp`, which every family shares). The line between the
experts the card copies and those the CPUs compute over a prompt is set from the medians of five readings rather than
one, which once sent every expert a prompt picked to the card. A model folder downloaded before a file of its program
existed is offered `lisp/` again.

## v0.4.0

A model's program travels with the model. A model folder carries it in `lisp/` — the Lisp the
evaluator runs the model with, which the boot reads from there: read it, change it, and the next boot runs what you
wrote. The packs on Hugging Face carry it, and for a model downloaded before, `silvann_core.py download-model <name>`
fetches `lisp/` alone. Sampling runs on the card, so a sampled answer comes about as fast as a greedy one (the 35B on
one card, answering a chat: from 75 to 101 tokens/s), and Qwen's answers are made on the card eight tokens at a time.
And the card's kernels are faster: on one MI50, at about 2,000 positions, the 35B answers at 87 tokens/s (from 38), the
27B at 30.5 (from 20.3), and Ministral 3 14B at 42 (`D4`) and 30 (`D8`) — measured now at a long prompt as the other
rows are; on a short prompt, 56 and 37 tokens/s, where v0.3 measured 52 and 26.

**Known issue:** a kernel update can leave the AMD driver unbuilt for the new kernel, and the machine then boots on the
kernel's own `amdgpu`, where ROCm runs but card-to-host copies corrupt memory (`free(): invalid pointer`, at random).
After a kernel update, `modinfo -n amdgpu` should name a file under `updates/dkms/`; if it does not, boot the previous
kernel, or install an `amdgpu-dkms` that builds for the new one.

## v0.3.3

A fix — on the CPU, a small buffer's header was written where its release did not read it, and
releasing it could unmap part of the process's memory; it showed as `free(): invalid pointer` at the end of a run, or
not at all. And the 27B and the 35B on one card, measured again with the test machine's cooling working (it had been
throttling the card): 20.3 and 38 tokens/s, from 17.4 and 37.

## v0.3.2

A fix — on a machine busy when the engine starts, the CPU's pool of threads could be sized to none,
and the experts on the CPU were then skipped without an error, so the answers were wrong. Upgrade if your experts run
on the CPU. And GLM 5.3 Flash on a 32 GB card, measured again with 24 GB of tier: 10.7 tokens/s.

## v0.3.1

Mistral AI's Ministral 3 14B (Apache 2.0) — the first model of a third family, in two packs; two more
packs, the 397B and GLM 5.3 Flash with its dense part at 8 bits; how close each pack comes to the original weights, as
perplexity and bits per byte ([`docs/results.md`](../docs/results.md#how-good-each-pack-is-perplexity)); GLM's experts on
the CPU added in the same order every run, so a prompt gives the same answer each time (with a card's tier, which experts
the card holds depends on when its copies land, and that can still change the last bits); Qwen's experts on the CPU
multiplied in integers (`experts_int8`), as GLM's already were; and for GLM an exclusive tier (`card_exclusive`), where
what the card holds is not also kept in RAM ([`docs/models.md`](../docs/models.md#the-cards-tier-of-experts)).
