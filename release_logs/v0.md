# SiLVANN 0.x — what each release brought

The releases of major version 0, newest first. Within a major an update keeps the models and the conversations
working; the README carries the newest release's notes, and this file every one of them.

## v0.16.0

DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash runs, GLM 5.3 Flash's exclusive tier runs over several cards and repeats from boot, and a model's
experts can be locked in RAM.

**DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash.** DeepSeek's 384-expert model, its `D8E4` pack 491 GB — 203 GB of it the n-gram memory's tables,
read from the disk a row at a time ([`docs/models.md`](../docs/models.md#deepseek-v41-flash)). Its experts run on the CPU,
as many layers in RAM as fit and the rest read from files written beside the pack; it reads a prompt as rows, puts a
conversation away and brings it back exactly, and calls the tool in its own format. Checked against DeepSeek's own code,
layer by layer over 1,300 positions. It reads text, up to 16,384 positions in this release: past them its candidate
blocks take over, and they are not here yet. Its rates come with v0.16.1.

**GLM over several cards.** A mask runs over the group, each card with its share; the tier is lent the cache's rows a
conversation has not reached; a prompt is read as rows. `card_shared` `"auto"`, the default, times a CPU's expert against
a card's at boot and keeps a shared slice only where the CPU is less than four times slower — on two Xeon E5-2680 v4
beside MI50s it is 12–15 times, so none. With `card_exclusive: true` every expert is on one card or in RAM, each card
swapping its own: the group gives back every card's tier of RAM
([`docs/config.md`](../docs/config.md#tensor-parallelism)).

**GLM's exclusive tier repeats from boot.** An exclusive tier's swaps now take effect at a set point rather than when
their copies land, so GLM's answer with one is the same from boot to boot, and it decodes faster: on an MI50, 8.2–8.3
tokens a second against 6.7–7.6 before.

**Experts locked in RAM.** `experts_lock: true` keeps the experts copied into RAM from being swapped out (Linux's
`mlock`) — the Qwen MoE models, GLM and DeepSeek — and the boot says how to raise the limit when it refuses. With swap on,
set `vm.swappiness` to 1 ([`docs/users.md`](../docs/users.md#the-experts-in-ram-and-swap)).

**Faster on the MI50.** Attention at heads of 512, the hyper-connections' first step and the indexer's choice have AMD
kernels of their own: GLM 5.3 Flash decodes at 7.00 tokens a second at 3,036 positions, from 6.11.

**OpenCL.** An OpenCL 1.2 card with memory of its own — a discrete card, not an integrated GPU — is sized as a card when
the server starts: the tier of experts and the context are chosen by trying them on it. On Windows, `install.ps1` now
builds both OpenCL families every time and sends an NVIDIA card older than compute 7.5 to OpenCL 1.2, as `install.sh`
does — checked against mocked cards, not yet on a Windows PC.

**The chat.** A dark mode, kept for the browser; a copy button on every block of code; and a long line in the thinking or
in a tool's section wraps.

**Docker.** No image is published: `docker/` builds one of your own.

## v0.15.1

Windows has its prebuilt libraries again, and an older NVIDIA card runs through OpenCL 1.2.

**Windows.** v0.15.0's Windows evaluator did not build, so its release carried no `silvann_windows_x64.zip` and
`install.ps1` built the libraries itself. This release carries them again.

**An older NVIDIA card, through OpenCL 1.2.** CUDA's family takes compute capability 7.5 and newer. A card older than
that — a GTX 10x0 — now runs through the OpenCL 1.2 family instead of the CPU, with nothing to install but the card's
driver. A developer preview: not yet checked on such a card.

**OpenCL built by every install.** On Linux both OpenCL families are built every time, against Khronos' headers carried
in the release, and need no package to build. At run time they take only a card no other family takes.

**The installer.** A source other than GitHub (`SILVANN_SOURCE`) is cloned with `git`, which it now asks for; a family
that does not build says why.

## v0.15.0

A mixture-of-experts model runs over several cards, GLM 5.3 Flash takes a mask, and a model whose experts are
copied into RAM needs much less memory to start.

**An MoE over several cards.** `tensor_parallel` now splits a mixture-of-experts model's attention by heads too: the Qwen
MoE models with their experts on the CPU, and GLM 5.3 Flash with its experts in RAM. Each card keeps its own tier of
experts, with a tenth of its slots holding experts every card has; a position's experts go to the cards that hold them,
and the rest to the CPU. On two Xeon E5-2680 v4 with MI50s, GLM answers in 109 ms a token on one card, 100.7 on two and
80.9–86.5 on four. It needs cards of one size that can read each other's memory, on AMD, and it is a developer preview
([`docs/config.md`](../docs/config.md#tensor-parallelism)).

**Less memory at the start.** A model whose experts are copied into RAM no longer keeps its weight files mapped beside
them. GLM 5.3 Flash's peak fell from 242 GB to 147 GB: on a 256 GB machine it no longer pushes its own experts out to
swap, which had held a few of its layers up by about 15 ms at a time.

**GLM takes a mask.** A rank-1 mask in GLM 5.3 Flash's `loras/`, as the Qwen models take one
([`docs/tools.md`](../docs/tools.md)): with its experts in RAM, on the card's tier, or read from the disk.

**The chat.** An answer can be up to 16,384 tokens long. A message sent while the model waits on its own question no
longer locks the conversation: the message stays in the box, and the question's buttons stay under the answer.

**Building.** The engine builds with GCC 13 (Ubuntu 24.04's), and `install.sh` names Python's headers (`python3-dev`)
when they are missing.

**Why AI on your own machine:** [`docs/local_ai.md`](../docs/local_ai.md), for the work that cannot leave the room.

## v0.14.0

SiLVANN reads a model's experts from its pack faster than from the file earlier releases wrote beside it, and an
integrated GPU reads the model's memory where it lies.

**The experts from the pack, faster.** An expert lies in a pack as several pieces; the loader's threads now read them
side by side rather than one after another. With `experts_from: "disk"`, on two Xeon E5-2680 v4 with an MI50 and a
SATA SSD, the 35B-A3B waits 6.8 ms a token on its reads against the old file's 8.2, and answers at 23.6–24.3 tokens a
second against 22.5–23.7 — the same logits, bit for bit. GLM 5.3 Flash's packs, uploaded again on 19 October with each
expert's records in the experts' order, are read in place too: a GLM pack downloaded before that day still writes its
file at the first start ([`docs/users.md`](../docs/users.md#5-the-experts-on-the-disk)).

**An integrated GPU, one copy.** Through the OpenCL 1.2 family, a GPU whose memory is the machine's RAM — a laptop's, an
APU's — no longer keeps a second copy of the memory the program shares with it, nor copies it across each token.
Checked through a CPU's OpenCL (PoCL), which shares its memory the same way; not yet on an integrated GPU itself.
`SILVANN_OPENCL1_UNIFIED=0` asks for the copies.

**macOS is not supported.**

## v0.13.0

GLM 5.3 Flash answers faster with its multi-token prediction layer, and Qwen 3.6's MTP layer can be turned on.

**GLM's MTP.** `mtp: "yes"` in a GLM config's options: the MTP layer drafts the token after the next one, and the model
checks the draft and the next token together — the same greedy answer, 1.18× through the server on two Xeon sockets
with the experts in RAM ([`docs/config.md`](../docs/config.md)). The GLM package is `ai_z__glm_5_3`. GLM's packs were
uploaded again with each expert's records in the experts' order and the router's bias from the checkpoint: read from
the disk, a new download's experts are read where the pack holds them.

**Qwen's MTP**, the Qwen 3.6 packs: `mtp: "yes"`, `"no"` by default — 1.12× with the experts on the CPU, 0.91× with them
on an MI50, where checking two positions still costs nearly two tokens' time.

**Faster on the MI50.** A layer's norm and the rotation after it are one launch (`rmsnorm_rotate`), the same bits.

**OpenCL 1.2 for older cards.** Every NVIDIA card CUDA 12 does not build for (Kepler to Volta), Intel's GPUs before
Gen9 and AMD's TeraScale and first GCN go to the OpenCL 1.2 family — a best guess from the vendors' published versions;
a card on the wrong one is an issue to open, with its `clinfo`.

## v0.12.0

SiLVANN runs natively on Windows — no WSL, no Docker — from the libraries the release was built
with, or built on the PC.

**Windows, natively.** The engine's calls to the operating system — threads, libraries, files, memory, the processors —
go through one layer, a folder a system (`engine/src/operating_systems/`), as its arithmetic goes through a silicon
family. `install.ps1` fetches the release, makes its Python environment and takes the libraries GitHub built for the
release (`silvann_windows_x64.zip`, on the release's page: the evaluator for Python 3.10 to 3.14, NVIDIA's family for
every card it lists, the CPU's, and the two OpenCL families), so Python, Git and the card's driver are all a PC needs.
`build.ps1` builds them on the PC instead, with Visual Studio's clang-cl and, for an NVIDIA card, CUDA's nvcc (12 or 13).
AMD's and Intel's GPUs go through their driver's OpenCL. The model's tool has `(powershell …)` beside `(bash …)`, and
`(help)` tells the model it runs on Windows ([`docs/tools.md`](../docs/tools.md)). The libraries are not signed yet: where
Windows 11's Smart App Control is on, Windows refuses them, and the installer says what to do
([`docs/users.md`](../docs/users.md#on-windows)). On an RTX 5070 Ti with a Ryzen 7 5800X,
built from source with CUDA 13.4, Ministral 3 14B `D4` on the card answers in the chat at 58 tokens a second.

**The experts read from the pack.** With `experts_from: "disk"` a Qwen model's experts are read where its pack holds
them: the first start no longer writes them out again beside it — 55 GB for the 122B, 16 GB for the 35B. The logits are
the file's to the bit; on the RTX 5060 Ti's desktop, from its NVMe drive, the 122B answers 2.7% and the 35B 3.4% below
the file's rate. Their `experts/` folder an earlier release wrote is not read any more on one socket and can be deleted.
GLM 5.3 Flash still writes its file: its pack keeps an expert's records in the order of their names
([`docs/users.md`](../docs/users.md#5-the-experts-on-the-disk)).

**No Docker image.** None is published from this release, and the scripts that ran one are gone: SiLVANN installs
natively on Linux and on Windows. `docker/` keeps the Dockerfile, for a container of your own
([`docker/README.md`](../docker/README.md)).

**In progress:** the engine carries the first part of DeepSeek V4.1 Flash's port (`ai_deepseek_4`); its pack is not
published yet.

## v0.11.1

The server answers as fast as the card does — v0.11.0's server drew each token with a slow top k on an NVIDIA card,
through OpenCL and on the CPU alone — and the chat follows the model the server serves.

**The sampler's top k on CUDA, the CPU and OpenCL 2.0.** The server draws each token with the model's sampler on the
card: its repetition penalty, temperature, top k and top p. In v0.11.0 CUDA, the CPU and OpenCL drew the top k with nn's
generic body, which reads the whole vocabulary once for each of the `k` picks: 13.8 ms a token for the 35B's 248,320
logits on the RTX 5060 Ti, nearly as long as the rest of the token. CUDA now has ROCm's top k, the CPU and OpenCL 2.0
one of their own, with the same picks and weights to the bit: 0.024 ms on the RTX 5060 Ti, 0.03 ms on the CPU alone,
0.89 ms on an MI50 through OpenCL. In the chat the 35B with its experts in RAM answers at 67 tokens a second on the RTX
5060 Ti, where v0.11.0 gave 32 to 35, and Ministral 3 14B `D4` at 47 where it gave 35 to 37; the 35B on the CPU alone at
10.5 against 8.4 to 8.7 on the RTX 5060 Ti's desktop, and on a short prompt at 16.6 against 11.4 on the R730; through
OpenCL on an MI50 at 29.9 against 11.9. The rates after a prompt, which take the most likely token, are as they were
([`docs/results.md`](../docs/results.md)).

**The chat follows the model.** When the server starts serving another model — from Options, or by anyone else — the
chat closes the conversation the last model made, says which model serves now and lists that model's conversations; a
model with none gets one, open and empty. In v0.11.0 the last model's conversation stayed in the chat. An answer running
holds the chat as it is: a subagent the card is lent to shows its own model on the server until the answer is done
([`docs/ui.md`](../docs/ui.md)).

**The docs.** The rates lead with the chat's — the server with the model's own config, as the chat's meter reads a
short conversation — and the rates after a 2,000-position prompt beside them, every RTX 5060 Ti row measured again with
v0.11.1; a new row, the 122B with its experts in 62 GB of RAM, answers in the chat at 20 tokens a second, against 8.5
with 32 GB and its experts read from the disk. [`docs/results.md`](../docs/results.md) is laid out by machine, each row
naming its version. On Linux the Docker image runs as fast as the install; on Windows well below what the same PC gives
under Linux, so there it is for evaluating SiLVANN ([`docker/README.md`](../docker/README.md)).

## v0.11.0

The model's tool grows into a workbench — it draws pictures, hands work to other models and keeps commands of its
own; a Qwen MoE model's context and its card's tier of experts share the card with nothing to set; and a dense
model's layers split over several AMD cards.

**Pictures from the tool.** A text model's REPL draws with the picture model: `(image "a lighthouse at dusk --size
768x1024 --seed 7")`. On the text model's own card the model is put down, the picture drawn and the model booted again
with its conversations where they were — back in 10 to 11 s on an MI50 and on the RTX 5060 Ti 16 GB — or the picture is
drawn on another card (`image_card`) and nothing is put down. The config's `image_model` says which picture model:
`"auto"`, a downloaded one, by default; without one the tool says so at once, and the model tells you. The picture is
shown in the conversation and kept with it, and it is the picture model's own: the same text and seed through the 27B's
tool and through Qwen-Image's server gave the same picture, every pixel. `--count 4` draws four, seeds s to s + 3, one a
card at once — on four MI50s in 120 s against 261 s on one card, the same pictures — in a picture model's messages too.

**Subagents.** `(agent 'model "config" "task")` opens a conversation with another model, or the same one under a LoRA,
and the model is then inside it as in a REPL: each call is a message, `(answer 'once)` or `(answer 'refuse)` answers the
subagent's ask — only what the caller could do itself without asking, anything more asked of you — and `(exit)` ends it.
It runs on the same server (`'self`), on a machine in the config's `workers`, or in place, the card lent to the other
model for each exchange; its REPL reads and lists only, unless its config allows more. `(whoami)` says which model this
is, its cards and context, its LoRAs with what each is for, and what `(agent …)` can reach: given a task, the 27B and the
35B chose the LoRA whose description fit it.

**The REPL.** `(reset)` starts it afresh — its names, its folder, a waiting ask — and keeps the files, the folders you
allowed, its mode and the library: definitions kept across conversations with `(keep 'name "what it does")`,
`(library)`, `(import 'name)` and `(forget 'name)`, in `tools_library.json` beside `tools.json`. The names the models
reached for and the REPL lacked now answer: `read-file`, `slurp-file`, `read-all`, `cat` and `slurp` are `read`;
`system`, `shell` and `sh` are `bash` — 18 of the 25 such calls in 159.

**Skills** — commands that live in folders: a `SKILL.md` names each command, what it touches (so it is asked as a
built-in command would be) and the Python function or the program that runs it, in the data folder's `skills/`. The
release ships one, `(find "folder" "*.txt")`. `(skills)` lists them, `(help)` names their commands, and `(guide)` gives
the model the tool's guide, `docs/tools.md`, so it can write a skill of its own and keep it: the 35B and the 27B each
did, from the guide alone.

**Tensor parallelism, opt-in, on AMD cards.** `"options": {"tensor_parallel": 2}` splits each layer of a dense Qwen
model over that many cards of one memory size: the 27B from 29.9 to 23.1 ms a token on two MI50s, a 2,000-position
prompt 1.8 times faster on two cards and 2.2 on four; refused for the mixture-of-experts models, over cards of unequal
memory, and on one card. Untested on NVIDIA cards. It needs `lisp/qwen3_5_tp.lisp` in the model's folder, on the Qwen
packs on Hugging Face: `silvann download` of a model already here fetches its pack's `lisp/` whole.

**No context to set, no tier to size.** Every model's `max_context` is `"auto"` by default now: as many positions as
the card holds beside the model, up to the model's own length, found when the server starts. With a mixture-of-experts
model's experts on the CPU, the card's tier of experts also holds experts in the cache's rows no conversation has
reached, giving them back 8,192 positions at a time before a conversation writes there, and taking them again for a
shorter one. On the RTX 5060 Ti 16 GB the 35B-A3B at 262,144 positions lends its tier 2.4 GB that way, and answers as
fast as a boot sized for the conversation: 54.0 tokens/s after 2,000 positions, 40.8 after 16,000 and 30.8 after
32,000, against 54.1, 40.7 and 30.8. GLM 5.3 Flash's tier is lent its latent cache's rows the same way: 11 GB on a 32 GB
MI50 at its whole 1,048,576 positions. Where the card cannot hold the whole length beside the model, the tier keeps the
least it takes, a slot a layer, and the context the rest: GLM on 16 GB of an MI50 790,528 positions and a 0.5 GB tier,
lent the rows a conversation has not reached. With `"auto"` the experts stay on a card that holds them with 32,768
positions or more. A config written before keeps the `max_context` it names: set it to `"auto"` in Options
([`docs/config.md`](../docs/config.md#the-context-and-the-tier-share-the-card)).

A model booted a second time in one process — the text model back after a picture, or after lending its card to a
subagent — no longer takes the first boot's expert copies still in flight into its tier: the 35B with a tier answered
another conversation wrongly after an `(image …)`, and now answers as if it had never been put down. A GLM conversation
past 2,048 positions, put away while another had the card and brought back, chose the positions it attends from the
other's indexer: a conversation keeps its indexer's caches with it now, and its next positions are those it would have
had never put away, to the bit.

**The docs and the chat.** [`docs/config.md`](../docs/config.md) — every key of a model's config, every family's options —
and [`docs/tools.md`](../docs/tools.md) — the REPL, the library and skills, step by step — are both in the UI's Help, and
the UI's New config writes the keys the server reads. The chat's top bar shows how full the conversation is: its tokens
of the model's context. An emoji split over two tokens is shown whole (it was "�"), and `kv` refuses a value it does not
know (any other ran the fp16 cache). The Help view lists every document the release ships, in four groups, first a
guide to the UI itself ([`docs/ui.md`](../docs/ui.md)), and a link from one document to another opens it there.

**The command and the setup.** `silvann models <folder>` keeps the models, the conversations and the rest SiLVANN keeps
for you in another folder from then on: SiLVANN on a USB drive, its models on the machine's own disk. `silvann help`
lists the commands, and a word it does not know says so. A setup kept in `autotune.json` is chosen again after an
update, which may change what a boot needs; and a tier's size named alone in a Qwen config puts the experts on the CPU
and chooses the rest, where it stopped the boot. `vram_margin_mb` (50 by default) is how much of each card the
setup chosen at boot leaves free for a desktop or a browser — `0` on a card nothing else uses; it was a fixed 256.

## v0.10.0

Pictures from a prompt — Qwen-Image 2.1 runs as a server's model of its own: a message is a prompt, and its answer a
picture.

[Qwen-Image 2.1](https://huggingface.co/eval-apply/silvann_tq_D8_qwen_image_2.1) (`silvann_tq_D8_qwen_image_2.1`, 18 GB)
is a 7-billion-parameter diffusion transformer with Qwen3-VL 8B's text model as its encoder: the two at 8 bits, its
picture decoder at 16, each on the card in turn, written in the engine's words as the text models are
(`engine/src/packages/ai_qwen_image`; its programs `lisp/qwen_image.lisp`, the transformer's steps, and
`lisp/qwen_image_vae.lisp`, the decoder's layers). In the UI a message typed is a prompt: its progress is
shown beside the box — the prompt read, each step, the decode — and the picture is drawn in the conversation and kept
with it as a PNG. A message sets its picture's size, steps and seed (`--size 768x1024 --steps 20 --seed 7`), the
config's `image` the rest. `silvann_cli` saves the picture where it runs, and through the API the answer's text names it.
A 1,024 x 1,024 picture at 40 steps takes 146 s on an RTX 5060 Ti 16 GB and 572 s on an MI50
([`docs/users.md`](../docs/users.md#a-picture-model-qwen-image-21)). ⚠ Its licence is the Qwen Research License Agreement:
research and other non-commercial use only.

Checked against diffusers' Qwen-Image 2.1 pipeline from the same prompt and seed, on both cards: a block of the
transformer, its prediction and six steps of it, the encoder's rows, the decoder, and the whole pipeline's latents and
pictures — each nearer diffusers' float32 than its own bfloat16 pipeline is. On the RTX 5060 Ti, through the server's
API and in a browser: the first conversation opened by itself, a prompt typed, its progress, the picture, the page
reloaded and the picture drawn again, a second picture.

The chat, after a user test: an answer is drawn as it comes without drawing the conversation again; while the model
thinks the answer says "thinking…", and **show thinking** shows the thought in a section of its own; a run of the
model's tool calls is a section, closed until you open it; a model with no conversation saved opens one as its server
starts, and a message typed brings it online without **Activate**. A picture model's header has no thinking or tool
switches.

On NVIDIA cards the CUDA family has its own kernels for what a picture needs: attention with a limit to each row's keys,
matrices at 16 bits (the decoder's 3 x 3 convolutions 30 to 48 times faster than the engine's generic kernel), a
convolution's rows, and an activation's rotation row by row before an 8-bit product. The 16-bit matrices and the
rotation serve the Qwen models' vision tower too: the 27B's reads a 1,024 x 1,024 picture in 0.65 s and a 2,048 x
2,048 one in 6.3 s on the RTX 5060 Ti. The AMD family has the first of them now as well, and reads a prompt's
DeltaNet rows without spilling registers: the 27B's 2,097-position prompt 3% faster on an MI50.
On Windows, NVIDIA's driver lets a model larger than the card start on it and then read the rest over PCIe at every
token: the CUDA family now refuses an allocation past the card's free memory, which has not yet
been checked on Windows; until it is, [`docs/users.md`](../docs/users.md#on-windows) says which config
keeps a model within the card.

## v0.9.0

Tool calling — a model opens a Lisp REPL on the machine it runs on, in its answer.

The model calls one tool, `lisp`, in the format it was trained on — Qwen's and GLM's `<tool_call>`, Ministral's
`[TOOL_CALLS]` — with a form to evaluate: the answer stops where the call ends, the form runs in a REPL beside the
server, and its value is read back in as the model's chat template frames a tool's answer, the answer going on from
there. `(help)` lists the commands: `ls`, `read`, `write`, `cd` and `pwd` for the folders; `bash`, a shell command in
the working folder (stopped, with what it started, after 60 seconds — up to 600 when the form asks — or after 1 MB of
output); `fetch`, a web page's words without its markup; and Lisp to put them together — `define`, `let`, `lambda`,
`map`, `filter`, text and lists. The UI shows a run of calls as one `<!tools>` block ([`docs/users.md`](../docs/users.md#the-models-tool)).

What a command may do without asking you is the conversation's, chosen beside its **tools** switch: ask first — reads in
the workspace and in folders you allowed; reads unasked — every read, the default (a config's `tools`, `"chill"`); full
auto — everything. Anything else stops the answer with a question under it — allow the folder, allow once, or refuse —
and a refusal is said to the model, which answers without it. The switch works as the thinking switch does: off, the
model's calls are answered that the tool is off; a conversation's first message declares the tool, so one begun without
it does not take it on later. `"tools": "no"` in a config turns it off for every conversation on that server. The REPL
starts in `workspace/` beside the conversations (`tools_folder` names another) and runs as the server's user — in the
Docker image, the container's.

In a terminal, `silvann_cli` (`silvann_cli.py`, in the release from this one) has the conversation the UI has — over
ssh, say: the answer written as it comes, its thinking dimmed, the tool's calls and their values shown, and an ask
answered at a prompt — `y` once, `f` the folder (a read), `n` or Enter refuses. It takes the server's defaults unless
`--notools` or `--nothink` says otherwise, and `/mode` chooses what the tool may do
([`docs/users.md`](../docs/users.md#3-serve-it-and-chat)).

Checked through the server's API on two MI50s: the 27B, the 35B-A3B, the 122B (`D4E4`), GLM 5.3 Flash and Ministral 3
14B (`D8`) each found a word in a note in its workspace through the tool; the 27B, the 35B-A3B, the 122B and GLM asked
before reading a file outside it and went on once allowed; the 27B told the user of a write refused, and the 122B's
`(bash "wc -l list.md")` ran once allowed. A call and its answer are framed as each model's chat template frames them,
to the token. The terminal's chat, on the 35B-A3B: a read outside the workspace unasked in "chill", a `bash` asked
and allowed with `y`, a read asked and refused with `n`, an answer stopped with Ctrl-C, and a conversation put away
after two idle minutes answering again. On an RTX 5060 Ti the same through the server for the 27B, the 35B-A3B, the
122B and GLM, and for the 27B and the 35B-A3B a `fetch` and a write asked too; the terminal's chat the same on the
35B-A3B; the UI's switch, its selector and the three answers to an ask clicked in a browser; and inside the Docker
image, the tool on the volume's `workspace/` and `docker exec -it silvann silvann_cli`.

⚠ The models have new names — `silvann_tq_<bits>_<model>`, the model's name in lowercase:
`silvann_tq_D8E4_qwen3.6_35b_a3b`, `silvann_tq_D4_qwen3.8_27b`, `silvann_tq_D4E4_glm_5.3_flash`,
`silvann_tq_D8_ministral_3_14b` and the rest (`silvann list`). A model downloaded with an earlier release keeps its

Also: two servers started on one machine boot one after the other, each trying the card as the other leaves it. A setup

## v0.8.0

Pipeline parallelism, and a model's setup chosen for the machine it starts on.

A ring of machines: a model's layers split over several PCs, each holding a run of them, each server started from its
own config — `pipeline_parallelism` "yes", its `pipeline_parallelism_layers` and `pipeline_parallelism_next`, the
machine after it; the one whose layers begin at 0 answers the UI, the one with the last layers runs the head
([`docs/users.md`](../docs/users.md)). A model larger than one machine's RAM or card runs over several: GLM 5.3 Flash
over two stages, its experts in their RAM, gives the whole model's tokens, 33 of 33, at 6.95 tokens a second on two
MI50s; the 27B over two stages the same, 33 of 33. A config's `card` picks a machine's card, or `"cpu"` its CPU and RAM
— so a card and the CPU beside it are two machines of one ring: on the RTX 5060 Ti, the 35B-A3B half on the card and
half in RAM answers as Hugging Face's does (7 of 8 tokens, then its second at its own near-tie) at 13.8 tokens a second.
What crosses the network is a row of numbers a position. A Qwen model's pictures are read on one machine for now.

A config that names no placement — where the experts sit, how much of the card holds the ones used most, how long a
conversation the card has room for — gets one when the server starts: the setups are tried on the card fastest first
(one the card cannot hold is refused in about 2 seconds), the RAM is measured — a container's or a service's memory
limit counts, and 500 MB is left spare beside what the server holds — and the choice is kept in the model's folder,
`autotune.json`, so the next start takes it without trying ([`docs/users.md`](../docs/users.md#4-the-models-settings)).
`max_context` "auto" fills the card, up to the model's own length; `card_experts` "yes" or "no" keeps or leaves out the
card's tier, sized in 256 MB steps; a placement the config names is started as it is. On an MI50 it gives the 122B with
80 GB of RAM a 25 GB tier — 24.6 tokens a second, as the 24 GB given by hand — and GLM with 150 GB a 21 GB tier, 9.2-9.4
against 7.9-8.1; on an RTX 5060 Ti with 28 GB of RAM, the 35B-A3B an 11 GB tier, 57 tokens a second, and the 122B and
GLM their experts from the disk, as their rows of the list. A server that cannot start says why, in one line in the UI's
Options, with the setting to change. When RAM cannot hold two conversations at the full context, the one put aside is
saved to its folder and read back when it returns.

A video is read to its budget: the frames the processor samples, each fitted as it is read — a minute of 1080p held 0.39
GB at the peak, from 10.48 — and `max_tokens_per_video` (8,192 by default) sets how many tokens one becomes. With the
experts on the CPU the server no longer grows as it answers (40 rounds had added half a gigabyte; now flat), and its
answers are the same to the bit.

The Docker image keeps what it holds across an update: a test updates a running app from one image to a newer one of the
same tag, over the same volume, and checks that the model answers, that the conversations — a picture's and a video's
among them — come back with their history and go on, and that the video reader and the token are still there; it passed
from v0.7.0's image to this one, and runs before every release. Its data is a folder: the desktop's **SiLVANN data**
(`silvann_open_data`) opens it in Explorer, and a model downloaded on another machine is pasted into its `models`; the
image gives everything in it to the app's user each time it starts. `silvann_reclaim_space` gives Windows back the room
a deleted model leaves in Docker Desktop's disk file. The Windows scripts make their folder whole at each run — an
action or a desktop shortcut an older install lacks. `SILVANN_NAME` and `SILVANN_VOLUME` keep a second SiLVANN beside
the first.

## v0.7.0

Pictures. The Qwen models read one — the 27B, the 35B-A3B and the 122B, whose packs carry the vision
tower: in the UI the 📎 button attaches it to a message, and over the server's API `/cartridges/<id>/images` reads it
and a message names it. The picture is prepared as Hugging Face's processor prepares it and read by the model's tower;
its tokens take the model's three-part positions (time, row and column: M-RoPE), which the library computes in a word of

two-axis rotary — over the pack's own codes, with the CUDA family's set attention on the tensor cores and the AMD
family's own: pictures up to 4 megapixels (2048 x 2048, 4,096 tokens), from a quarter of a second for a small one to
about 15 s at the largest on an RTX 5060 Ti, and from 0.13 s to about 8.5 s on an MI50. Its weights (0.6 GB) stay on the card when the experts are on the CPU and in RAM otherwise, and its working
memory on the card is sized at boot for the largest picture — the 27B at its default context still fits a 16 GB card
(`picture_pixels`, `picture_weights`: [`docs/users.md`](../docs/users.md)). Checked against Hugging Face on the same
weights: the tower's output within 0.5% (Hugging Face's own tower in bfloat16 is 2.5% from its float32), and over a
picture's prompt the first attention layer within 6e-4. A conversation keeps its pictures, and is saved and brought back
with them. GLM 5.3 Flash reads pictures too, through the tower its pack carries (24 blocks of its own design, in the

float32, whose own bfloat16 is 7.6% away). Ministral 3 reads none: its pack carries no tower.

Videos, for the Qwen models and GLM: the same button attaches one — an animated GIF, WebP or PNG as it is, any other
video file through PyAV, which `install.sh` installs from `requirements-nonfree.txt` and the Docker image leaves out
(its scripts' `install_nonfree` adds it: [`docker/README.md`](../docker/README.md)). Its frames are taken at 2 a second, two frames read
together as one picture by the tower, each pair's time written into the message as the model's processor writes it; a
video is made at most half the conversation's context in tokens. Checked against Hugging Face's processor and tower on
the same weights — the frames, their times, the prompt to the token, the Qwen models' positions, the tower's rows
within 6e-4 — and a 6-second video's digits named in order by the 27B, the 35B-A3B and GLM.

Files, for every model: a Word document (`.docx`), a PDF (through pypdf, which the install brings) or a text file
(Markdown, CSV, JSON, code), attached with the same button, is read when it is attached, and its text goes into the
message before what you type, cut with a note to what the conversation's context still holds.

A model's program travels with it: the pictures' part is a file of its own, `lisp/qwen3_5_pictures.lisp`, which a folder fetched

A Docker image, for Windows above all ([`docker/README.md`](../docker/README.md)). It is the release, its environment and

## v0.6.0

A family for old integrated GPUs, `khronos_opencl1_igpu`: the library's kernels as OpenCL C 1.2, for
a GPU whose OpenCL has neither shared virtual memory nor the generic address space. The build works out every pointer's
address space itself, so the library's source names none. It has run on one device so far, the RTX 5060 Ti through
NVIDIA's OpenCL: the library's checks pass, and the 27B answers as Hugging Face does, at 1.6 tokens a second. On a card
with memory of its own, the RAM the card reads crosses the bus every token; an integrated GPU, whose memory is the
machine's, is what it is for. It takes a device only once the checks have passed on it, and so far that is the 5060 Ti
alone, which the CUDA family takes first: no integrated GPU runs it yet
([`engine/src/silicon_families/khronos_opencl1_igpu/README.md`](../engine/src/silicon_families/khronos_opencl1_igpu/README.md)).
And a model holds less of the machine's RAM: it loads its output head and its embedding table before its layers and
keeps no copy of its own once the engine has them. Ministral 3 14B on the CPU alone holds 7.4 GB, from 10.0, and needs
7.4 GB while it loads, from 10.6; with a card, the 27B holds 0.3 GB of the machine's RAM, from 5.9 (beside the pack's
pages the system caches, which it gives back when it needs them).

## v0.4.0

A model's program travels with the model. A model folder carries it in `lisp/` — the Lisp the
evaluator runs the model with, which the boot reads from there: read it, change it, and the next boot runs what you
wrote. The packs on Hugging Face carry it, and for a model downloaded before, `silvann_core.py download-model <name>`
fetches `lisp/` alone. Sampling runs on the card, so a sampled answer comes about as fast as a greedy one (the 35B on
one card, answering a chat: from 75 to 101 tokens/s), and Qwen's answers are made on the card eight tokens at a time.
And the card's kernels are faster: on one MI50, at about 2,000 positions, the 35B answers at 87 tokens/s (from 38), the
27B at 30.5 (from 20.3), and Ministral 3 14B at 42 (`D4`) and 30 (`D8`) — measured now at a long prompt as the other
rows are; on a short prompt, 56 and 37 tokens/s, where v0.3 measured 52 and 26.

**Known issue:** a kernel update can leave the AMD driver unbuilt for the new kernel, and the machine then boots on the
kernel's own `amdgpu`, where ROCm runs but card-to-host copies corrupt memory (`free(): invalid pointer`, at random).
After a kernel update, `modinfo -n amdgpu` should name a file under `updates/dkms/`; if it does not, boot the previous
kernel, or install an `amdgpu-dkms` that builds for the new one.
