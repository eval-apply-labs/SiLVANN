# SiLVANN 1.x — what each release brought

The releases of major version 1, newest first. Within a major an update keeps the models and the conversations working.
The README links here for what is new; the 0.x releases are in [`v0.md`](v0.md).

## v1.1.0

The portable app on Linux as well as Windows, one folder for both; the Lisp at a prompt with no model, the
interpreter's and the model's tool's, and one syntax for the two; a mask beside the card's tier of experts. The engine
gains a verb, so its libraries are built again and Windows' are signed again; the models and the conversations of 1.0.x
keep working.

**One portable app, for Windows and Linux.** `silvann_portable_x64.zip` replaces `silvann_portable_windows_x64.zip` and
unzips to three folders: `windows\` (double-click `SiLVANN.exe`), `linux/` (run `SiLVANN.sh`, on 64-bit x86 with glibc
2.28 or later) and `data/`, the models and conversations of both. On a USB drive formatted exFAT, one folder carries
them from a Windows PC to a Linux one and back, each system starting its own Python and libraries on the same data.
On Linux the window is Chrome, Chromium, Edge or Brave in app mode where one is installed, else the default browser
with a message box standing for the window; closing it saves the conversations and ends everything the app started, as
on Windows, and so does the app ended another way. `linux/SiLVANN.sh --menu` puts it in the desktop's applications
menu. From 1.0.x's portable folder, move its `data\` — beside `SiLVANN.exe` there — beside `windows\` in the new one
([`users.md`](../users.md#the-portable-app-on-windows-and-linux)).

**Two REPLs that need no model.** `silvann_base_repl` boots the Eval-Apply Generalized Interpreter on the CPU in a
fraction of a second and keeps it up between forms: a form at its prompt, a file, `-e FORM`, or another program's forms
through a pipe, a line a form, as a program drives `java`. An error answers `#<error W>` and the machine is kept; a call
it cannot make stops the machine (`#<panic W>`), and `,reboot` boots a new one; Ctrl-C ends the form running, not the
machine; `,verbs` lists the verbs. `silvann_tools_repl` is the model's tool at your prompt, its commands, skills and
kept definitions over the same workspace the models use, and it asks you what a model would ask its user. `install.sh`,
`install.ps1` and both container images put the two on the PATH
([`developers.md`](../developers.md#the-interpreter-at-a-prompt), [`tools.md`](../tools.md#the-same-repl-at-your-prompt)).

**One syntax for the two Lisps.** The engine's and the tool's read one core the same: `quote`, `if`, `begin`, `let`,
`set!`, `define`, `defun`, `while`, `and`, `or`, numbers, `#t`, `#f`, `nil`, `+ - * < > = eq?`. `define` binds in the
frame it runs in, as in Scheme: at the prompt a name defined again is still one binding, and inside a `let` or a
procedure the definition goes with it. `set!` changes a name something bound and refuses one nothing did; `=` is `eq?`,
so `(= 1 1.0)` is `#f`; `+ - *` of more than two numbers work left to right in both, so a sum of floats rounds the same.
Where the two part, and why there are two: the engine's scoping is dynamic and the tool's lexical, the engine's tests
take only `#t` or `#f` and its arithmetic one kind of number at a time, and text and `lambda` are the tool's
([`two_evaluators.md`](../two_evaluators.md)).

**The engine from the tool.** `(engine '(form))` runs a form in an interpreter the tool's session keeps: the session's
values go with it, a `define` there stays for the next call, and the files' verbs are refused. A form calling what the
engine cannot call (`progn`, `lambda`, a procedure of the tool's, a list in a call's head) is refused before it goes, with the engine's word for
it, so the failure that would stop the engine stops nothing. Common Lisp's `(defun name (x) …)` is answered with the
form rewritten, in both, and `(help 'defun)` gives its shape. At the base REPL, `,tools FORM` runs a form by the tool.

**The engine.** `sys__bindings__define`, a verb of sys's: set the name where the environment holds it, add it where it
does not. `define` and `defun` lower to it, so a long session defining a name again holds one binding, not one a
definition. `add-bindings`, `remove-bindings` and `unquote` are the engine's own and refused at a prompt. The models'
programs read as before: against Hugging Face the 2B and the 35B give 1.0.3's tokens, to the same distances. An update
builds the libraries again; GitHub's Windows libraries and the portable app are signed by Eval Apply Consulting LTD.

**A mask on a release install.** A rank-1 mask (a config's `lora` naming one) did not boot on a release install, on
any model: the package that reads its file, `safetensors`, was not among the requirements. It is now, and an update
installs it. A `lora` whose folder is missing, holds neither `mask.json` nor `pack.json`, or holds a mask's
`mask.json` without its `mask.safetensors` is refused in words that say which.

**A mask beside the card's tier of experts.** On Qwen's MoE models with their experts on the CPU, a mask and the card's
tier of the experts used most were refused together — and the setup chosen at boot gives a card too small for the
model a tier, so with a mask such a model did not start unless its config named no tier. The tier now computes the
picks it holds with the mask's terms, as GLM's does: the card keeps its own copy of the mask's part for the routed
experts, about 10 MB for the 35B. On the 35B with a mask, the run with a tier gives the same top token as the run
without one at every position.

**What is loading.** A model whose experts the CPU runs loaded them after its weights on the weights' line, which
climbed from 0 to 100 a second time and read as a load going round again. The experts have a line of their own,
`loading experts: …`, in the server's log and in the UI's pane.

**The public beta, and a hardware report.** The README, [`users.md`](../users.md) and `CONTRIBUTING.md` say what the
beta is: the normal path has been tested on a reasonable sample of cards, but a bug-free experience is not expected
yet. Issues → **Hardware report** is a form for a setup the release was not tested on: the version and install, the
system, the cards and driver, the model, what you ran, the numbers and what the server printed.

## v1.0.3

A patch on 1.0.2, whose engine and libraries it keeps as they are: an update says what it is doing, and the server
passes over a folder it may not read.

**An update says what it is doing.** A model's update reads the files it has no record of — a folder copied here from
another machine is read whole the first time — and said nothing until it was done. The UI's bar now runs twice:
**checking…**, the bytes read against the pack's hashes, then **downloading…**, the bytes fetched; in a terminal,
`silvann download` says the checking's rate and the time left every ten seconds. A file the pack has replaced since it
was fetched here is fetched without being read first. `silvann update` says pip's packages as it fetches and installs
them, each silicon family as it is built, "still building" each minute while one takes long, and each step's time, on
Linux and on Windows.

**A folder the server may not read.** A models folder on a disk of its own holds the disk's `lost+found`, which only
root may read: the REPL's `(whoami)` stopped there with a `PermissionError`, and so did a refusal naming the models an
agent could reach. Such a folder is passed over.

**The documents.** [`docs/inspiration.md`](../inspiration.md): the router beside attention's collapse — the meaning
chosen among the experts too, the computation centred near the manifold; the KV cache's tape as the conversation both
attentions steer, the person's experience mapped onto the weights' frozen hyperstition; the philosophy alone as lenses.

## v1.0.2

A patch on 1.0.1, whose engine and libraries it keeps as they are: the UI's own scripts run, so the chat follows an
answer and a code block has its copy button; a model's load says what is loading.

**The chat follows the answer.** v1.0.1 was to follow an answer while you are at the bottom of the chat and to stay
where you scrolled otherwise; it stayed everywhere, at the bottom too. The page adds its scripts once the browser has
connected, and a script added to a page already drawn does not run: the follow was never there, and neither was the
copy button on code blocks. Both are now in every page as it is first sent. The chat follows an answer from its first
token while you are at the bottom; a scroll up leaves the view where you put it, and back at the bottom it follows
again. Each code block has its **copy** button.

**What is loading.** A model's load said `loading: ...10...20`, and a model with an n-gram memory — Flash-Next,
DeepSeek — climbed a second time on the same line as its tables were read. It now says `loading weights: …` and, on a
line of its own, `loading engrams: …`, in the server's log and in the UI's pane.

**The documents.** [`docs/inspiration.md`](../inspiration.md)'s angles: Land's later patchwork beside his singularity —
exit without voice, residents sorting into enclaves, many projections each in a room of its own — and a negotiation as
where they are put back together ([`local_ai.md`](../local_ai.md#as-a-dipl-o-mat)); a bubble's elites weighed by the
same facts, losing members to a bad system or the bubble to a bad reading of the real; Bayes' rule written whole.

## v1.0.1

A patch on 1.0.0, whose engine and libraries it keeps as they are: on Windows, the documents say what the first signed
release did, and the installer refuses Windows' own folder and says when a data folder is gone; in the UI, an answer
goes on when you leave its page, and the chat stays where you scrolled.

**Signed, and Smart App Control lets it run.** From v1.0.0 the libraries GitHub builds for a release — the evaluator
and the silicon families — and the portable app's `SiLVANN.exe` are signed by Eval Apply Consulting LTD. On a PC with
Windows 11's Smart App Control on, the portable app and the installer's libraries served a model, and the README and
[`docs/users.md`](../users.md#on-windows) no longer say they are refused. Libraries built on the PC are not signed, and
Smart App Control may still refuse them; what to do then is in the same section.

**The installer on Windows.** `install.ps1` installs under the folder it is run in, and an administrator's PowerShell
starts in `C:\Windows\System32`: it now refuses Windows' own folder, and says to run it from your own PowerShell. A
data folder on a disk since removed or wiped — named by `silvann data` or by `SILVANN_DATA` — stopped it with
PowerShell's "cannot find drive" before the models' programs were checked; it now says which folder is gone and how to
name another, and finishes.

**Leaving a page mid-answer.** A page left while an answer was being written — another address in its tab, or the
tab closed — came back frozen: the answer went on into a copy of the browser's storage the UI had already closed, the
page that came back never showed it, and the UI could stop on an `IndexError` when the conversation changed under it.
The answer now lives in the UI while it is written, and every page of that browser shows it: back in the same tab or in
a new one, it is still being written there, and the conversation is yours to go on with. The server put a conversation
away two minutes after its page last asked, even mid-answer; now it never puts away one being answered, and counts from
the answer's end. With one conversation on the card at a time, the one you left stays there until you open another;
with seats, one whose page has gone is put away 20 seconds after its last answer, which frees its seat — the UI tells
the server when a tab's last page goes (`POST /sessions/gone`). And the chat follows an answer only while you are at
the bottom: scrolled up to read the thinking, the view stays where you put it ([`docs/users.md`](../users.md),
[`docs/ui.md`](../ui.md)).

**The documents.** [`docs/inspiration.md`](../inspiration.md) adds why the angles have to stay many: a CT scanner
reconstructs nothing from one angle, so a singularity is a scanner that keeps a single one; a viewpoint earns its
weight by what it predicts, as Bayes' rule weighs a frame and a scanner's statistical reconstruction its rays; and
models trained on what earlier models generated lose the rare first.

## v1.0.0

The first release of major version 1: 0.29.1's engine, with the models and the conversations of every 0.x release
carried over as they are. An old NVIDIA card reads a prompt twice as fast through OpenCL, and the documents say what
SiLVANN is built on, what inspired it, and where Eval Apply Labs stands.

**The containers' major.** `:1-amd` and `:1-nvidia` are the newest release of major version 1, and `docker/setup.sh`
pulls them; `:0-amd` and `:0-nvidia` stay 0.29.1. A container `docker/setup.sh` made moves to 1.0.0 with its `update`,
fetched again as the README's line does, over the same folder ([`docker/README.md`](../../docker/README.md)).

**Old NVIDIA cards read a prompt faster.** The OpenCL 1.2 families — `nvidia_opencl1`, for the NVIDIA cards older than
CUDA's family takes, and `intel_opencl1_igpu` — add the routed experts' weighted sum over a prompt with a kernel of
their own: the tile the experts' matrices already ran on, each sum added onto the running one in nn's own expression,
where nn's generic kernel ran before. On a Tesla V100 the 35B reads a prompt at 160 positions a second, from 75, its
sums the same to the bit.

**The documents.** [`docs/concepts.md`](../concepts.md) is now the concepts the engine is built on — the interpreter and
the library, the engine as SICP's evaluator, how it is extended — and a new [`docs/inspiration.md`](../inspiration.md)
what inspired them: meaning as a negotiation, the map people agree on, pain and the loss, and the conversation where the
negotiation reopens. In [`docs/local_ai.md`](../local_ai.md), "As a Dipl-O-Mat" answers its hardest objection, that a
clearer-headed counterpart is also a more capable one, and "How SiLVANN positions itself" says what Eval Apply Labs
publishes — high-quality packs — and why the packer is not: a statement of intent rather than a guardrail.
